# The Dip Protocol & The Rip Protocol

Two mechanical formulas distilled from everything tested in this project
(dip atlas: 4,751 episodes; 20+ experiments; 4 sealed QC verdicts). These
are NOT alpha machines — the alpha versions died honest deaths. They are
the surviving discipline: harvest the equity risk premium at better prices,
never cap your winners, never ride a broken thesis. Their value is that you
will actually execute during panic, which most people don't.

---

## BUY THE DIP

**Rule 1 — Ladder, never bottom-call.** Dips are memoryless: at −20% there
is a coin-flip it reaches −30%, at −30% a coin-flip to −40% (measured at
every depth). So deploy reserved cash in tranches on the MARKET (SPY or a
quality index), automatically:
  - SPY −10% from its 252-day high → deploy 25% of reserve
  - SPY −20% → deploy 35%
  - SPY −30% → deploy the last 40%
No prediction, no indicator, no waiting for "stabilization" — tested: RSI
recross fires ~31 days early with 61% false bottoms; timing adds nothing.

**Rule 2 — Individual names only in market-wide panic.** Single-stock dip
buys require ALL of:
  - SPY ≥ 12% below its high AND VIX > 30 (systemic — the best-recovering
    class; a lone stock bleeding in a calm market is the WORST class)
  - the name is down ≥ 30% from its high
  - the business is intact: profitable, manageable debt, and the crash
    story is fear/flows — NOT fraud, going-concern, or structural decline
    (the AppLovin-vs-TradeDesk check: growth intact + small miss = fear;
    slowest-growth-ever + guidance declining = broken. Skip broken.)
  - buy in 2 tranches: half now, half only if it falls another 15%.

**Rule 3 — Schedule buys into the turn-of-month window** (last 4 / first 3
trading days). Costless, measured positive in both decades.

**Rule 4 — Size for the coin-flip.** Max 5% of portfolio per name. Assume
every dip level has ~50% odds of one more −10% leg, because it does.

**Never:** average below your planned tranches; buy idio-grind knives;
buy any name whose crash IS the business; use tight stops near lows
(stops just under the low get wicked out 52% of the time).

## SELL THE RIP

**Rule 1 — You sell for risk control, never for cleverness.** Profit
targets at "resistance"/prior highs tested WORSE than holding: they cap
the +50–200% tail that pays for every loser. No selling just because it
hit a round number or an old high.

**Rule 2 — The trim (the actual sell-the-rip).** When a rip pushes any
position above 1.5× its target weight (a 5% position grows past 7.5% of
the portfolio), sell it back down to target. Mechanical, emotionless — you
harvest strength without decapitating the winner.

**Rule 3 — The trailing stop.** Exit a dip-buy entirely if it closes 20%
below its highest close since entry. Never tighter — 20% trailing beat
both tight stops and fixed targets in testing.

**Rule 4 — Thesis-break sells 100%, immediately.** If the fundamentals
break — guidance implying revenue decline, structural share loss, the
customer story failing — sell regardless of the chart. The market being
"due for a bounce" is not a thesis.

**Rule 5 — Graduation.** When a dip-buy regains its old high, it is no
longer a dip trade. Either it earns a place as a normal holding on its own
thesis, or trim to target. No autopilot holding.

**Rule 6 — The tax nudge (US).** Short-term gains are taxed as income.
When a trim/stop decision is marginal and the position is approaching 12
months, the after-tax math often favors waiting — let the calendar, not
emotion, break ties.

---

**What these are:** discipline for harvesting the market's risk premium at
good prices, with tested risk control. **What these are not:** an edge
over the index — every timing/selection layer we added was tested and
subtracted value. The formulas' power is that they fire when your emotions
won't.
